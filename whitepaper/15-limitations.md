# Limitations

Open Backlog is small on purpose, and that costs something:

- **It is for people who use git.** Someone who has never used git needs a tool, a tracker mirror
  or a colleague to take part.
- **Three statuses only.** No "in review" or "blocked" (section 7). Custom status folders are an
  idea for later, not a promise.
- **No notifications, no real time.** People see changes after a pull, and may hit a merge
  conflict.
- **Permissions are all or nothing:** the repository's (section 14).
- **Questions across many items need a program**, since nothing is stored that can be worked out
  (section 7). Very large backlogs will want a cache, never committed.
- **Reports are only as good as the commits** (section 11).
- **Links across backlogs are text**, checked only when the other backlog is cloned, and `parent`
  never crosses backlogs.
- **IDs show where work was filed, not where it is**, and parallel branches can collide on one
  (sections 7 and 9).
- **Attachments are not part of the format.** Images and logs can live in the repository, but make
  it larger forever.
- **It is new:** a 0.x draft that may break before 1.0, young tools, one real backlog, and one
  maintainer (section 16).
